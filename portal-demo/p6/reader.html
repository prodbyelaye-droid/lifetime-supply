Raw action solves all. That’s the rule this whole guide runs on, and I mean that literally.

Every single tactic in the next eighteen chapters runs on one thing, which is you actually doing it. None of it works for the producer who’s sitting around waiting to feel ready. So before we get into the store or the paperwork or the placements you need to get the rules down first.

the rule

Your output comes from discipline and it doesn’t come from your mood or from inspiration or from having a good week.

Discipline is the operating system, and your feelings don’t get a vote on whether the work happens. You’re going to have days where every beat sounds like nothing and the inbox is completely dead. Those days count exactly the same as the good ones because the upload still goes out and the pack still gets sent.

How you do anything is how you do everything. If the work only happens when you feel like it you’ve really just got a hobby with a store attached & that’s not what this guide is for.

my days

I went full-time as a teenager from Orange, which is a regional town in New South Wales with about forty thousand people in it. No studios, no industry and certainly nobody around me doing anything like this.

Before I had a single credit a normal day looked like this. I was cooking up every day and trying to learn as much as humanly possible about every VST I used, down to every setting and every option. I was absorbing everything I could find on YouTube. I stayed active in every group chat I was in and I built a connection with every producer I was talking to.

Then there was the outbound side. I was sending samples out every two days, sending packs to A&Rs for credits, booking calls with people and just grinding DMs.

That’s what attached to my name long before any plaque did. People knew me as the guy who was extremely good at networking and jumped on every opportunity as it came & I didn’t let any of them slip.

None of that needed talent and none of it cost money. It just needed me to show up every day.

ship now

Overthinking costs you money and you should honestly count it in dollars.

A pack that sits in a folder for three weeks because one loop needs another bounce earns you nothing for three weeks. An upload you hold back for the perfect Friday misses four Fridays. Meanwhile the producer who shipped the rough version already has the plays, the feedback and the reply you’re still waiting to deserve.

There’s no perfect time. There’s now and there’s later, and later is where most producers keep their whole catalogue.

So finish it, label it, send it and fix the next one.
